## 1. Purpose

`Kinetic Narrative Frame V1` defines the channel's distinctive visual language around one principle:

> A generated image is not a paused illustration. It is a narrative frame that contains the trace of the previous action and the directional force of the next action.

The channel must not depend on photoreal historical reconstruction, hero portraits, detailed object inspection, or static scenic beauty to hold attention.

Instead, each image should make the viewer feel that the story is already moving before the video animation begins.

The system therefore prioritizes:

```text
STORY FLOW
> KINETIC DIRECTION
> NARRATIVE RELATION
> SPATIAL STRUCTURE
> CHARACTER / EVIDENCE / SYMBOL
> SURFACE DETAIL
```

A frame succeeds when the viewer can infer:

1. what has just happened,
2. what is happening now,
3. where the story is likely to move next.

---

## 2. Channel signature

### 2.1 Name

`KINETIC_NARRATIVE_FRAME_V1`

### 2.2 Short statement

> **Follow the story, not the object.**

### 2.3 Long statement

The channel does not ask the viewer to stop and inspect a person, weapon, building, artifact, costume, or landscape for its own sake. People, objects, evidence, architecture, weather, graphics, and environments are used as carriers of narrative movement.

The visual frame should remain in motion conceptually even when the source is a still image.

### 2.4 Six channel axioms

```text
1. STORY OVER DETAIL
2. MOTION OVER STILLNESS
3. FLOW OVER FIXATION
4. DIRECTION OVER POSE
5. STRUCTURE OVER SPECTACLE
6. CONTINUATION OVER CLOSURE
```

These axioms are channel-level. They apply across Roman history, Joseon history, legends, mysteries, wars, archaeology, biographies, folklore, and other approved history/mystery subjects.

---

## 3. Paradigm shift

### 3.1 Previous default risk

A conventional historical-generation workflow tends to ask:

```text
What did this place look like?
What did this person wear?
What did this artifact look like?
How cinematic can this reconstruction become?
```

This encourages:

- photoreal or film-still convergence,
- over-detailed armor / costume / face / architecture,
- long visual fixation on one subject,
- historical verification disputes around details that are not essential to the story,
- weak image-to-image momentum,
- generic cinematic-history aesthetics that are difficult to own as a channel identity.

### 3.2 New default question

`Kinetic Narrative Frame V1` asks instead:

> **What must the viewer feel moving, changing, approaching, separating, disappearing, colliding, or remaining unresolved at this exact narrative beat?**

The image form is chosen only after that question is answered.

### 3.3 Consequence

A Roman story does not require every frame to be a Roman-period reconstruction.

A frame may use, where factually appropriate:

- distant reconstruction,
- compressed spatial storytelling,
- a road or route as narrative structure,
- negative space,
- map-like spatial abstraction,
- symbolic but non-deceptive transition,
- evidence in context,
- parallel possibilities,
- temporal compression,
- silhouettes or simplified figures,
- architecture as directional pressure,
- landscape as narrative movement.

The rule is not "always depict the era." The rule is:

> **Use the visual form that advances the approved story most clearly without turning unsupported detail into apparent fact.**

## 4. Kinetic frame anatomy

Every designed frame should contain at least four of the following six components.

### 4.1 INCOMING TRACE

Visual evidence of what preceded this frame.

Examples:

- footprints,
- disturbed grass,
- open gate,
- fading smoke,
- trailing procession,
- partially crossed bridge,
- receding figures,
- shadow entering the frame,
- torn or displaced material,
- visible route already travelled.

The incoming trace prevents the frame from feeling like an isolated poster.

### 4.2 PRESENT ACTION

The current narrative beat.

It may be subtle. A frame does not need an explosion, battle, or dramatic gesture.

Examples:

- moving north,
- entering a passage,
- separating from a group,
- discovering a trace,
- approaching a gate,
- turning away,
- a path dividing,
- a record ending,
- a room closing around a figure.

### 4.3 KINETIC AXIS

The dominant direction through which the eye travels.

Possible axes:

```text
LEFT_TO_RIGHT
RIGHT_TO_LEFT
NEAR_TO_FAR
FAR_TO_NEAR
LOW_TO_HIGH
HIGH_TO_LOW
CURVED_PATH
CONVERGING
DIVERGING
CIRCULAR_PRESSURE
```

There should normally be one dominant axis and no more than one meaningful secondary axis.

### 4.4 MOTION CARRIERS

Elements that make motion readable without literal animation.

Preferred carriers include:

- roads,
- corridors,
- shorelines,
- rivers,
- formations,
- cloth,
- smoke,
- dust,
- rain,
- snow,
- grass,
- waves,
- shadows,
- doors,
- birds,
- crowds,
- light bands,
- architectural perspective,
- repeated vertical or diagonal forms,
- partially obscured foreground elements.

Motion carriers should belong naturally to the scene where possible. Literal comic speed lines are not a default channel device.

### 4.5 OUTGOING CUE

A visible invitation toward the next beat.

Examples:

- road leaving the frame,
- figure looking outside the frame,
- doorway not yet entered,
- distant structure,
- unresolved branch,
- empty area into which motion continues,
- evidence pointing toward another location,
- shadow or light leading beyond the visible scene.

### 4.6 OPEN EDGE

At least one side of the composition should conceptually remain open when the story continues.

The frame should not always resolve into a centered subject with balanced closure.

Open edges help create the sensation that the world and story extend outside the image.

## 5. Static image must imply time

A Kinetic Narrative Frame represents more than a single frozen instant.

It should suggest a short time envelope:

```text
T-1  previous action / trace
T0   current visual beat
T+1  next directional possibility
```

A viewer should be able to imagine at least one plausible frame immediately before and immediately after the image.

Reject a frame when it reads only as:

- a person posing,
- a building portrait,
- an artifact product shot,
- a scenic wallpaper,
- a symmetrical tableau,
- a generic establishing image with no next-direction cue.

---

## 6. Narrative distance principle

### 6.1 Default philosophy

> **See the event from far enough away that relationships are more important than decorative detail.**

The channel should prefer the distance at which the viewer understands:

- who is moving relative to what,
- what space is opening or closing,
- what direction matters,
- where the uncertainty lies,
- what changed between beats.

### 6.2 Subject scale

Default subject scale:

```text
SMALL
SMALL_TO_MEDIUM
MEDIUM only when narrative function requires it
```

Large subject dominance is exceptional.

### 6.3 Close-up rule

A close-up is allowed only when it changes the story.

Approved reasons may include:

- decisive evidence,
- irreversible emotional transition,
- critical recognition,
- specific source evidence that must be examined,
- a brief contrast beat before returning to a wider story space.

A close-up must not exist merely because a face, artifact, costume, weapon, inscription, or object looks impressive.

### 6.4 Verification-load rule

> **The closer and more specific the image, the greater the factual claim burden.**

Therefore detail level should be proportional to both:

```text
NARRATIVE NECESSITY
x
EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE
```

If either is low, visual specificity should decrease.

This rule is intended to reduce unnecessary historical-detail disputes without weakening the factual backbone of the story.

---

## 7. Character philosophy

Characters are narrative vectors, not mandatory portrait subjects.

### 7.1 Default character behavior

- readable silhouette,
- clear body direction,
- readable group relation,
- simplified face when distant,
- historically plausible broad clothing / equipment logic,
- no unnecessary microdetail,
- no default hero pose,
- no beauty portrait treatment.

### 7.2 Character function

A character should primarily communicate one or more of:

```text
DIRECTION
SCALE
ISOLATION
PRESSURE
PURSUIT
SEPARATION
ARRIVAL
DEPARTURE
REACTION
RELATIONSHIP
```

### 7.3 Recurring identity

When a recurring documented or approved character requires continuity, identity remains important, but identity must still serve the narrative beat rather than turn each shot into a portrait showcase.

---

## 8. Environment philosophy

The environment is not decorative background.

It is a narrative mechanism.

Preferred environmental functions include:

```text
PATH
BARRIER
FUNNEL
THRESHOLD
PRESSURE
SCALE
SEPARATION
DESTINATION
ABSENCE
UNCERTAINTY
```

Examples:

- a corridor can compress a character,
- a mountain pass can direct a force,
- a road can become the story's vector,
- a coastline can mark a boundary,
- an empty field can communicate absence,
- multiple roads can represent competing hypotheses,
- a door sequence can communicate progressive confinement.

The environment must not become an elaborate scenic painting that slows the story without adding narrative function.

---

## 9. Evidence philosophy

Evidence supports motion in the story rather than stopping it.

### 9.1 Default rule

> **Show what the evidence changes, not only what the evidence looks like.**

For example, an inscription does not automatically require a full-screen stone close-up. It may be shown as a trace within a larger archaeological or narrative space if the exact inscription is not the visual point of the beat.

### 9.2 Historical text

- Generated readable historical text remains forbidden.
- Unsupported exact inscriptions remain forbidden.
- Authentic supplied evidence may be used according to provenance and rights rules.
- Reconstructed evidence must not masquerade as authenticated primary evidence.

### 9.3 Evidence transition

After evidence is introduced, the visual flow should normally move toward:

- consequence,
- interpretation,
- contradiction,
- new direction,
- widened context,
- unresolved question.

The sequence should not become a gallery of isolated artifact close-ups.

## 10. Kinetic narrative beat types

The following beat types are the core vocabulary of V1.

### MOVE

The story changes location, direction, scale, or position.

Visual goal:

- readable trajectory,
- strong spatial axis,
- visible continuation.

### REVEAL

New information becomes perceptible.

Visual goal:

- concealment-to-visibility structure,
- partial opening,
- controlled attention shift.

### PRESSURE

The world visually constrains a person, group, possibility, or interpretation.

Visual goal:

- converging geometry,
- narrowing space,
- repeated barriers,
- opposing flow.

### COLLISION

Two forces, interpretations, groups, or facts meet.

Visual goal:

- opposing vectors,
- intersecting geometry,
- visual tension without default spectacle.

### BREAK

A known sequence, record, route, relationship, or certainty stops.

Visual goal:

- interrupted direction,
- missing continuation,
- abrupt negative space,
- broken rhythm.

### BRANCH

Multiple plausible routes or interpretations emerge.

Visual goal:

- controlled divergence,
- no unsupported single-answer emphasis,
- balanced or intentionally weighted alternatives according to evidence confidence.

### ABSENCE

The important information is what is no longer visible or documented.

Visual goal:

- meaningful emptiness,
- missing expected element,
- residual trace,
- scale that makes absence legible.

### QUESTION

The sequence deliberately remains unresolved.

Visual goal:

- open edge,
- distant or incomplete destination,
- continuation beyond frame,
- visual invitation rather than definitive answer.

## 11. Frame-to-frame continuity

Kinetic Narrative Frame is sequence-first.

A successful image should hand something usable to the next image.

At least two of the following should normally survive a handoff:

```text
KINETIC_AXIS
TRAVEL_DIRECTION
DEPTH_AXIS
LIGHT_DIRECTION
WEATHER_FLOW
ARCHITECTURAL_GEOMETRY
DOMINANT MASS
HANDOFF_ANCHOR
MOTION_CARRIER
COLOR / VALUE MASS
```

The next frame may transform these elements rather than copy them literally.

Example:

```text
road direction
→ corridor direction
→ text-line direction
→ coastline direction
```

The literal object changes, but the viewer's visual momentum continues.

This is preferred over matching images only by recurring objects or characters.

## 12. Image-to-video clip contract

A Kinetic Narrative Frame must be designed before generation with future clip motion in mind.

Each frame should eventually be able to expose structured fields equivalent to:

```text
narrativeBeatType
incomingTrace
kineticAxis
motionCarriers[]
presentAction
outgoingCue
openEdge
handoffAnchor
nextBeatIntent
```

These fields are conceptual in V1. They are not yet a database/schema change in `TASK-KNF-01`.

### 12.1 Clip motion rule

The video motion should **amplify the movement already implied by the frame**, not invent an unrelated cinematic move.

Good examples:

- follow the path direction,
- measured lateral track with marching flow,
- forward drift toward a threshold,
- reveal along an existing occlusion,
- parallax that strengthens depth direction,
- environmental flow continuing the composition,
- transition through the frame's open edge.

### 12.2 Avoid by default

- generic push-in on every image,
- face zoom because a character exists,
- orbit around static subjects,
- random drone motion,
- decorative crash zoom,
- one-object inspection that outlasts its narrative value,
- repeated camera moves that ignore the image's internal direction.

### 12.3 Anti-linger rule

The visual focus must change when the narrative relation changes.

A single image or clip should not remain on the same visual subject merely because the narration continues.

This does **not** impose one universal duration in seconds. Duration remains dependent on narration, format, editing rhythm, and provider capability.

The controlling rule is:

> **One visual focus should not outlive the narrative beat it serves.**

## 13. Surface and style freedom

Kinetic Narrative Frame is not defined by photorealism.

Channel identity should come primarily from:

- kinetic composition,
- story distance,
- directional structure,
- open-frame continuation,
- narrative beat design,
- sequence rhythm.

Therefore the rendering surface may be stylized.

Preferred broad direction:

```text
illustrated
matte
simplified where useful
graphically readable
layer-friendly
historically plausible at the required confidence level
```

Not required:

- photographic skin,
- cinema-lens simulation,
- PBR realism,
- hyper-detailed materials,
- film-still finish.

Avoid:

- cute / chibi treatment,
- toy-like miniature look unless explicitly justified,
- glossy game-render surface,
- generic fantasy concept art,
- visual novelty that obscures historical meaning.

No fixed decorative palette is defined by V1.

---

## 14. Graphic abstraction policy

Graphic or symbolic devices may be used when they improve story flow, but must remain subordinate to meaning.

Allowed examples:

- simplified route shape,
- fading directional trace,
- spatial compression,
- layered terrain abstraction,
- controlled negative space,
- visual branching,
- architectural repetition,
- non-textual temporal progression.

Not allowed by default:

- unsupported readable labels,
- decorative HUD/UI,
- arbitrary arrows everywhere,
- modern infographic language that breaks historical immersion without narrative reason,
- graphics that imply certainty not supported by the story.

The goal is not to turn the channel into an infographic channel.

The goal is to allow the frame to reorganize reality when necessary to communicate the story more clearly.

---

## 15. Historical factuality and uncertainty

Kinetic composition does not relax factual standards.

It changes where specificity is spent.

The approved hierarchy remains:

```text
FACT
RECONSTRUCTION
HYPOTHESIS
LEGEND
UNKNOWN
```

### FACT

Movement may be clear where the historical relation is supported.

### RECONSTRUCTION

Action and environment may be plausibly visualized, but the reconstruction must remain recognizably illustrative rather than authenticated evidence.

### HYPOTHESIS

Use divergence, distance, partial information, competing directions, or reduced specificity.

### LEGEND

Symbolic movement may become stronger, but the legendary layer must not be confused with documentary evidence.

### UNKNOWN

Favor interrupted motion, disappearance of the trace, unresolved direction, negative space, and open continuation rather than invented answers.

---

## 16. Anti-patterns

Reject or redesign frames that exhibit any of the following without explicit narrative justification.

### STATIC HERO

One large centered character dominates the frame with no meaningful spatial movement.

### OBJECT ALTAR

A weapon, artifact, crown, inscription, or prop is presented as a glamorous centerpiece unrelated to progression.

### TOURISM FRAME

The environment is beautiful but narratively inert.

### HISTORY POSTER

Characters are arranged as a promotional poster rather than participating in an ongoing event.

### CINEMA-STILL IMITATION

Photographic realism and dramatic lighting become the primary appeal while narrative direction remains weak.

### DETAIL TRAP

The image introduces unnecessary specific costume, emblem, inscription, weapon, architecture, or object detail that creates factual claim burden without improving the story.

### GENERIC PUSH-IN BAIT

The image only works if the eventual clip performs a slow zoom toward the center.

### CLOSED FRAME

Every visual force terminates inside the composition and no next-beat energy remains.

### ATMOSPHERE LOOP

Fog, sunset, rain, smoke, or darkness are repeatedly used as substitutes for actual narrative movement.

### SAME-SCALE FATIGUE

A sequence repeats the same wide/medium framing without changing narrative relation or visual energy.

---

## 17. Sequence rhythm

The channel should not seek constant visual intensity.

Kinetic storytelling requires variation.

A possible sequence may be:

```text
MOVE
→ REVEAL
→ PRESSURE
→ BREAK
→ BRANCH
→ ABSENCE
→ QUESTION
```

Another may be:

```text
MOVE
→ COLLISION
→ REVEAL
→ MOVE
→ BREAK
→ QUESTION
```

The important rule is that successive frames should not all perform the same visual function.

Visual rhythm should change through:

- scale,
- direction,
- density,
- openness,
- number of subjects,
- foreground obstruction,
- depth,
- motion carrier,
- certainty mode.

---

## 18. Long-form-first behavior

The final system remains long-form-first.

Kinetic Narrative Frame therefore must support sustained sequences, not only high-energy short-form montages.

For long-form:

- movement can be measured rather than frantic,
- open-frame direction can persist across multiple shots,
- repeated spatial motifs may evolve gradually,
- quiet frames are allowed if they still contain narrative continuation,
- editing rhythm should follow argument and story structure rather than constant novelty.

For short-form:

- the same paradigm may compress beats,
- visual direction must remain readable in vertical framing,
- critical motion and story information should remain near the central safe region,
- peripheral areas can carry atmosphere or continuation cues.

Format-specific implementation remains the responsibility of Format Profiles.

> Does the evidence advance the story rather than freeze it?

### KNF_SAMPLE_03 — BREAK / ABSENCE

Narrative idea:

- the previously readable path, formation, or record ceases to continue,

### KNF_SAMPLE_04 — BRANCH / QUESTION

Narrative idea:

- multiple possibilities continue from the unresolved fate,
- no route is presented as settled fact.

Test question:

These samples should be assessed as a sequence, not only as four independent artworks.

## 21. Validation rubric

A candidate sequence passes only if it performs well across all of the following.

### STORY READABILITY

Can the viewer understand the narrative change without inspecting small detail?

### KINETIC READABILITY

Does each still contain a clear sense of direction, pressure, interruption, or continuation?

### NEXT-BEAT PULL

Does the frame create a reason to continue watching?

### NON-FIXATION

Does the sequence avoid becoming stuck on a face, object, artifact, or location?

### DISTINCTIVE CHANNEL IDENTITY

Does the sequence feel meaningfully different from generic cinematic historical reconstruction?

### FACTUAL RESTRAINT

Does the design avoid unnecessary specific claims and preserve uncertainty where required?

### I2V READINESS

Can plausible clip motion be derived from the image's existing structure?

### SEQUENCE VARIATION

Do consecutive frames change visual function rather than repeat the same composition?

### ERA TRANSFERABILITY

Could the paradigm work for Roman, Joseon, legend, archaeology, or other history/mystery topics without losing channel identity?

### LONG-FORM VIABILITY

Can the system sustain a full sequence without becoming visually exhausting or gimmicky?
